What's built, and where it's headed

The social network for people who cook — Facebook and LinkedIn, built for the kitchen.

Cookzer is a social platform built for people who cook — home cooks and food enthusiasts sharing what they made, saving what works, planning the week ahead, and following other cooks whose taste they trust. Every major platform treats cooking as content to scroll past; Cookzer is where cooks actually live, and it's already up and running.

The reframe

This isn't a recipe app with a feed bolted on.

It's a social network for an entire industry — home cooks, aspiring chefs, and hospitality professionals — that happens to start with recipes, the same way Facebook started with a directory and LinkedIn started with a résumé.

Facebookgave people a wall, a feed, and friends. Cookzer gives cooks a feed of what people actually made, real reactions, and a network that grows around shared taste.
LinkedInturned a résumé into a professional network. Cookzer's Kitchen CV turns a cooking history into a portfolio hospitality employers and brands can actually evaluate.
Cookpadproved 60M+ people want a place for recipes — and validated that this category monetizes (65% subscription, 25% ads). It never built the social graph, messaging, or professional layer.
Home cooks
Share what they cooked, get inspiration, save recipes that work, plan the week ahead.
Aspiring & semi-pro cooks
Build a following, run weekly challenges, start turning cooking into a side business.
Hospitality professionals
A Kitchen CV mode turns a real cooking history into a portfolio — the "LinkedIn for chefs" half of the thesis.

Commercial model

Two ways this makes money — the deliberate focus right now.

Both ride on top of something already built and live: the feed for ads, and Pantry Challenge / Leftovers / Health for Cookzer+. Three more proven models sit below as deliberate future streams — not diluting focus across five at once, but not off the table either.

01
Ads & content sponsorships
Native sponsored posts and recipes from CPG and appliance brands in the feed, plus sponsored Weekly Challenges where a kitchenware or grocery brand underwrites the week's theme and prize — content people engage with, not a banner they scroll past. The revenue engine for every year of the model before Cookzer+ exists, and the larger of the two streams through Year 5.
Food creators see 18.4% engagement vs. typical feed content — brands spent ~$3B on creators in 2025, up 35% YoY. Modelled at Year 1's $1.67 blended ARPU (100% ads-driven that year), that implies roughly 17 sponsored impressions per user per month at an $8 CPM — under one every weekday, a conservative ad load. Holding that exact ad load flat all the way to Year 10's 15M MAU is already ~$25M/year from ads alone, before any increase in density or CPM as the creator ecosystem matures.
02
Cookzer+ AI Assistant
A conversational assistant across the three highest-intent surfaces on the platform — Pantry Challenge, Leftovers, and Health — sold as a Cookzer+ monthly subscription. Ask it what to cook from what's on hand, what to do with leftovers before they're wasted, or how to hit a nutrition goal, grounded in the user's own real recipes and cook-in history rather than a generic model. The second stream, and the one with room to grow fastest once the backend exists.
$5/month, 500 messages included (Claude Haiku 4.5) — ~$3.11 net profit per subscriber/mo after LLM cost and Stripe fees, a ~62% margin even at worst-case full usage. No delivery partner cut, no inventory. Modelled at a conservative <1% subscriber attach rate, it's already worth $7.2M/year by Year 10 inside the revenue model above (see Growth section) — live today as a waitlist-capture teaser on all three surfaces; the paid chat itself needs an LLM API integration and billing, both scoped, not yet built
Future revenue streams

Real, proven models the platform already has the infrastructure to support — deliberately not the near-term focus while Ads and Cookzer+ prove out first, not because they don't work. Revisit once those two are running at scale.

03
Future stream
Grocery cart connection
An "Add ingredients to cart" button on any recipe or shopping list, integrated directly with major grocery retailers — Woolworths and Coles in Australia — down to independent local shops in markets the majors don't dominate. Cookzer takes a commission on every order it originates.
Recipe-driven carts average +3 items vs. non-recipe shopping. Needs a retailer integration per market — a BD/partnership motion, not a build call.
04
Future stream
Ready-made meal plans
A paid meal-plan tier in the vein of Youfoodz or Weight Watchers — personalized weekly plans paired with ready-made delivery or a guided cook-it-yourself track, sold directly through the app people already use to plan their week.
WW International alone guided ~$695–700M in 2025 revenue from meal-plan subscriptions. Needs a delivery/logistics partner (or enough planner maturity to sell the guided track alone).
05
Future stream
Restaurant delivery & paid listings
Cookzer's receipt-verified restaurant module connects to delivery partners like Uber Eats and DoorDash for one-tap ordering — or, where that doesn't fit, restaurants pay directly for a featured menu placement in the directory.
Delivery marketplaces already charge restaurants 15–30% per order. Needs a delivery-partner integration or a direct restaurant sales motion — neither started.
