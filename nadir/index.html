What's built, and where it's headed

The social network for people who cook — Facebook and LinkedIn, built for the kitchen.

Cookzer is a social platform built for people who cook — home cooks and food enthusiasts sharing what they made, saving what works, planning the week ahead, and following other cooks whose taste they trust. Every major platform treats cooking as content to scroll past; Cookzer is where cooks actually live, and it's already up and running.

The reframe

This isn't a recipe app with a feed bolted on.

It's a social network for an entire industry — home cooks, aspiring chefs, and hospitality professionals — that happens to start with recipes, the same way Facebook started with a directory and LinkedIn started with a résumé.

Facebookgave people a wall, a feed, and friends. Cookzer gives cooks a feed of what people actually made, real reactions, and a network that grows around shared taste.
LinkedInturned a résumé into a professional network. Cookzer's Kitchen CV turns a cooking history into a portfolio hospitality employers and brands can actually evaluate.
Cookpadproved 60M+ people want a place for recipes — and validated that this category monetizes (65% subscription, 25% ads). It never built the social graph, messaging, or professional layer.
Home cooks
Share what they cooked, get inspiration, save recipes that work, plan the week ahead.
Aspiring & semi-pro cooks
Build a following, run weekly challenges, start turning cooking into a side business.
Hospitality professionals
A Kitchen CV mode turns a real cooking history into a portfolio — the "LinkedIn for chefs" half of the thesis.

Commercial model

Several ways this makes money — none of them require inventing a new behavior.

Every stream below rides on top of something already built and live: the feed, the recipe engine, the challenge system, or the Kitchen CV professional layer.

01
Brand advertising & sponsored recipes
Native sponsored posts and recipes from CPG and appliance brands, placed directly in the feed — not a generic banner, a recipe someone actually wants to cook.
Food creators see 18.4% engagement vs. typical feed content
02
Shoppable recipes & cart affiliate revenue
An "Add ingredients to cart" button on any recipe, integrated directly with major grocery retailers — Woolworths and Coles in Australia — so the basket checks out on their platform while Cookzer takes a commission on every order it originates.
Recipe-driven carts average +3 items vs. non-recipe shopping
03
Restaurant listings & featured placement
Cookzer's receipt-verified ratings directory gives restaurants a featured-placement slot to pay for — visibility built on real, proven visits instead of managed reviews they can buy anywhere else.
Yelp's restaurant & retail ad segment alone did $444M in 2025 — restaurants already pay for listing visibility
04
Kitchen CV Pro subscription
A paid tier for semi-pro cooks and hospitality professionals: portfolio analytics, priority visibility, an ad-free feed, and advanced planning tools.
Cookpad's closest comparable: 65% of all revenue is subscription
Longer horizon
Aggregate trend data licensing
Anonymized, aggregated data on what's trending by region and season — a genuine demand-forecasting signal CPG brands and retailers don't have anywhere else, once the network reaches real scale.
Longer horizon
Home-cook & tiffin marketplace
Let top-rated home cooks turn their reputation into income — list a tiffin subscription or home-cooked meal service, take orders, and get discovered by the same feed that already proved they can cook. Already scoped in the product roadmap, held back deliberately until the network has real density.
Longer horizon
Ready-made meal brand listings
A dedicated, paid placement slot for ready-made meal delivery brands — in the vein of YouFoods — to advertise directly to an audience already planning what to cook this week, alongside the informal home-cook and tiffin sellers on the same marketplace.
